# Assumptions and deviations

Decisions made without asking (per the working rules), with the reason for each. Anything here is a one-line change in `backend/config.yaml` unless stated otherwise.

## City and data

Timezone Asia/Kolkata | The team lives there. One radius would cover 30 km of empty land; zones keep the bundle small (2.5 MB) and full detail where people are. |

The raw files are regenerated by the scripts. |
| Overpass | Main `overpass-api.de` was at capacity during the build; config lists mirrors (mail.ru, kumi) used in order with a ≥1 s gap, backoff and an identifying User-Agent. One cached query. | Fair use. No live Overpass calls ever happen at runtime. |
| OSM metadata | POIs fetched with `out meta` to get each element's **last edit time** (used to age opening hours). Editor names/ids are dropped. | Lets the freshness engine age real data; no personal data kept. |
| Lighting | Only **2.5 %** of street length has an OSM `lit` tag and there are 21 street-lamp nodes. Tagged/lamped = KNOWN; untagged *main* roads = PROBABLE prior (risk 0.15–0.6); everything else = **UNKNOWN**, carried as a wide interval (0.25–0.9), never as a value. | The spec's "never present missing lighting as fact". |
| Opening hours | Only 35 of 371 facilities carry `opening_hours`. A small in-house parser handles `24/7`, day ranges, multiple time ranges, ranges past midnight and `off`; anything else is UNKNOWN. Tag age ≤ 1 y = KNOWN, ≤ 3 y = PROBABLE, older = OUTDATED. | Avoids a parser dependency without Python 3.14 wheels; graceful failure. "Last edit" is per element, not per tag (OSM limitation). |
| Category priors | Police and fire posts 24/7 (PROBABLE); hospitals 24/7 (stronger if `emergency=yes`, none for eye/dental/maternity/clinic-style names); ATMs 24/7 (weak); clinics and banks closed at night. **No prior** for pharmacies, food, shops, fuel: these stay UNKNOWN. | "Inferred from category = PROBABLE, no usable prior = UNKNOWN." |

| Weather | Real Open-Meteo hourly forecast, cached (`inferred`, TTL 48 h). Live "observations" are **simulated** around that forecast (synthetic), so rain/fog can be injected. Live refresh is a button (`POST /api/weather/refresh`), never automatic. | Real data where it exists; synthetic only where we must inject events. |
| Incidents | A seeded synthetic history (about 3,500 incidents over 90 nights) is biased by real OSM proxies (unknown/unlit lighting, distance to open places, industrial land). ## Freshness model

* **Status rules** are exactly SPEC §5, plus one extra: an observed fact older than **10 × TTL** becomes UNKNOWN (too old to use at all). That gives the full progression KNOWN → PROBABLE → OUTDATED → UNKNOWN as a feed stays dead.
* **Confidence decay** uses `reliability × exp(−age / (decay_scale × ttl))`, capped by kind (observed 1.0, scheduled 0.8, inferred 0.65, prior 0.4) and halved when OUTDATED. `decay_scale` defaults to **3**: with the literal `exp(−age/ttl)` a fact that has just turned PROBABLE already sits at 0.31 × reliability, and at 2 × TTL it is at about 11 %, which reads as broken. Set `freshness.decay_scale: 1` for the literal formula (a unit test covers both).
* **Route-level status of a category** (e.g. lighting along a route) is the weakest status that covers ≥ 30 % of the length (`coverage_threshold`), so a few unknown metres do not poison a route but a third of it does.
* **Plan headline trust** is the weakest link over the plan's **critical** dependencies, and its confidence is their product:
  * walk: closures + weather
  * bus/metro: transit timing + closures
  * cab: cab availability + traffic + closures

  Safety data (lighting, isolation, incident data, emergency access) is shown per plan with its own badges and **widens the safety interval**, but does not set the headline. With 97 % of lighting unknown, including it would pin every route at UNKNOWN and hide the changes that outages cause. The UI also shows `overall` (the weakest of everything).
* **Conflicts**: when realtime says a bus is cancelled or more than 5 min off schedule, the timetable fact is marked `contradicted_by` the newer observation (OUTDATED), the planner uses the observation, and the plan carries a "Conflicting sources" warning.
* **Debounce**: a KNOWN↔PROBABLE flip must persist for two ticks before it is written to the change log (one dropped reading is noise). Transitions to or from OUTDATED/UNKNOWN are logged immediately.

## Planner

* **Departure time is fixed** at the requested time. Once it passes, the trip is "in progress (assumed you left on time)" and is re-evaluated with current data. There is no GPS tracking. A request for a time that has already passed plans from now. Plans freeze 10 min after the worst-case arrival.
* **ETA triples** propagate segment by segment. Walking is 1.45 / 1.25 / 1.0 m/s, slowed by rain. Cab rides use per-road traffic facts with bands that widen by status; when traffic is UNKNOWN the ride uses `[free-flow, 2 × free-flow]` (there is no historical speed profile in this build, so nothing is invented). Bus waits come from realtime (KNOWN ±, PROBABLE wider), else the timetable (−1 / +8 min), and a possibly-cancelled trip is skipped in the worst case.
* **Transit** is single-ride (walk → board → ride → alight → walk) over the best board/alight stop pairs on the same route. That is equivalent to board/alight virtual edges for transfer-free trips. Transfers are not modelled.
* **Candidates**: walking shortest paths at λ ∈ {0, 0.5, 1, 2, 4} with edge cost `time + λ × 0.8 s/m × risk × length`, plus one penalised alternative, de-duplicated at 85 % overlap. Cab: fastest roads, and main roads with a light risk weighting. Bus/metro: up to 4 stop-pair combinations. The previously recommended route is always re-evaluated as well.
* **Selection** follows SPEC §6.4: the feasible pool is chosen by worst case, then expected, then best. *Recommended* maximises `rw × safety_lo/100 + (1 − rw) × 0.6 × normalised ETA`, where `rw` is the risk slider (default 0.7, so robust safety first with an ETA tie-break). *Fastest*, *Safest* and *Balanced* are labelled as specified. Other modes are shown as *Alternative* with explicit warnings, so users can see why they lost.
* **Risk**: the six components each give `[lo, hi]` per edge, are length/time weighted, use configurable weights, and are scaled 0.8-1.2 by minutes spent outdoors. Inside a cab, lighting, isolation and incident risk are multiplied by 0.35; on a bus, by 0.3. Waiting inside a safe haven caps the risk.
* **Closures**: barricades and accidents block **vehicles**; pedestrians may pass but take a risk penalty. Waterlogging blocks everyone. A closure fact that has gone UNKNOWN (very old) widens risk instead of blocking.
* **Hysteresis**: the plan switches only when the current plan breaks (closure, cancelled trip), falls to a worse deadline case, has critical data that degrades to OUTDATED/UNKNOWN while the alternative is better, or is beaten by ≥ 5 worst-case safety points or ≥ 5 min at similar safety. Every keep or switch is logged with the reason.

## Product scope

* **SOS and trusted-contact sharing are simulated**; nothing is sent anywhere. The share link reopens the live plan on the same server.
* **LLM (Groq)** is optional and only turns the user's own words into fields (times, place names, modes). Places are resolved against the offline OSM gazetteer, never by the LLM. Explanations are template-generated from structured facts; LLM rewording was deliberately left out so no number can be invented.
* **Need-now** checks open-state **at the time you would arrive**, lists UNKNOWN-hours places without ever calling them open, and offers a probably-open hospital as the fallback for medicine.
* **No authentication**, and CORS is open: this is a local demo.
* Built and tested on **Python 3.14** (spec: 3.11+) and Node 25.
* **Demo video**: `scripts/record_demo.py` records an automated walkthrough with Playwright (a backup, not a narrated video).

## Android build (SPEC v3): engine on the phone

* **The engine is a TypeScript port** of `backend/app/core` + `presentation/simple_view.py` in `frontend/src/engine/`. The Python backend stays as the reference implementation and is not shipped. The same 8 scenarios run against both (`backend` pytest, `frontend` Vitest). Thresholds live in `frontend/src/config.ts`.
* **Area = zones, not a radius** (UPGRADE U1). `meta.center` / `radius_m` only enclose the zones (projection origin, grid extent); "in the area" means inside a zone or within 300 m of one. GPS outside the zones → the demo start point (IIIT Nagpur). The IIIT Nagpur permanent campus is not named in OSM; **Waranga village (20.946, 79.043)**, next to it, stands in (confirmed by the team, 2026-10-26: start IIIT Nagpur = Waranga, Home = Dharampeth, Work = Sitabuldi). The drive graph is derived from the walk download (one Overpass query less).
* **One walking graph is used for cars too** (edges flagged `carOk` by highway class). One-way streets are ignored for cabs: a small ETA error on a 4 km area, and it halves the bundle. Documented, not hidden: the cab ETA band is already wide.
* **Simple-view safety thresholds are 66 / 55** on the worst-case score, not 75 / 50. With 97 % of lighting UNKNOWN, worst-case scores sit lower than the spec's examples assumed; 75 made every night walk "careful". The tests pin the boundary values.
* **Cab wait is a prior model only** (`cab.baseWaitMin` by hour). The simulator's cab-shortage truth is never read by the planner; it would only reach it through a feed, and no cab feed exists.
* **Local time** uses a fixed offset (`tz_offset_min` 330, IST has no DST), so the app gives the same answers whatever the phone's timezone.
* **`npm run data`** runs `tools/build_data.py` with `backend/.venv` Python when present (`scripts/py.mjs`). Laptop-only; the APK only ships its output.
* **Engine in a Web Worker** (`src/engine/worker.ts`); the UI calls it only through `src/lib/engine.ts`. It pushes a fresh plain view on every tick (2 s) and only when it changed. The technical plan set is not sent to the screens; the Demo drawer asks for it separately.
* **App clock starts at the phone's real time**, running at 1x. Night logic uses this clock; the Demo drawer can set it (e.g. 2:15 AM). If the trip is planned after the deadline has passed, the app says "Too late" rather than moving the deadline.
* **Live / Offline pill**: Live = the phone is online **and** a real Open-Meteo `current` probe answered in the last 30 min **and** live information is coming in (not all switched off in Demo). Otherwise the engine is told the phone is offline: simulated live feeds stop, facts age, and plans run on saved info. A real reading is used only when the app clock is within 30 min after it (not when the demo clock is moved).
* **Map** (changed on request, see CHANGES.md #1): online, OpenStreetMap street tiles turned into a night palette (as in the web version), light use with attribution; the app identifies itself in its User-Agent (`NightWay-hackathon/0.1`). Until tiles load, after 3 failed tiles, or offline, the tiles are hidden and only the road network bundled in the app is drawn (major roads brighter), so the map never depends on the network. This deviates from the spec's "no downloaded tiles" only while online.
* **Last plan after a restart**: the app saves what was asked (from, to, when, by), the shown option and whether the trip started, then re-plans on start-up with current information and picks the same option. It does not restore a stale snapshot.
* **My location**: Capacitor Geolocation with a 4-5 s limit; outside the demo area (or no fix / no permission) it uses the demo start point and says "(demo)".

## Help (SOS), dispatch, mesh, safety timer (Phase 4)

* **Who is asked first** depends on the button: *I'm in danger* and *Not sure* → police, then hospital; *I need a doctor* → hospital, then police (`dispatch.ladder` in config). Each desk gets `ackTimeoutS` (20 s in the demo) before the next is asked. After the last desk the status is **Not sure** and the app keeps listening, so a late acknowledgement still shows **Help is aware**. Call 112 is always on screen.
* **"Passed on"** means the request left the phone: the server accepted it (direct), or a nearby phone received it (mesh). **"Help is aware"** only after a desk pressed Acknowledge.
* **SOS packets carry the desk** (`to`) in addition to the spec's fields, so an escalation can travel through the mesh too. In BLE (Phase 7) it fits in 2 spare bits of the type byte.
* **Simulated mesh**: 6 virtual phones in a fixed layout where the gateway is 3 hops away, so multi-hop, duplicate drops and the hop limit are visible. The gateway posts with the phone's own network. So in airplane mode the request is stored and goes out when signal returns, which is honest store-and-forward. The Demo drawer can force "Help via mesh" and switch the gateway's signal off.
* **Text my contact** opens the SMS app with the message and an OpenStreetMap link filled in; the user picks the contact and taps send (no contact list access, nothing sent automatically). Location = the trip's current point, else the start point.
* **Safety timer**: "Are you home?" at `max(Be there by, trip start) + 10 min` (app clock, converted to real time so a 10x/60x demo clock works). It is shown as a local notification and in the app. No answer within 5 min → a simulated alert to the server's **Contact** desk and the app offers the text. Switching on is remembered. End trip / I'm home cancels it.
* **Plain http to the laptop**: the APK allows cleartext traffic (`usesCleartextTraffic`) because the demo server is on the local Wi-Fi without TLS. The dispatch URL can be baked in at build time (`VITE_DISPATCH_URL`) or set in the Demo drawer (saved on the phone).
* **Demo clock jumps re-plan the trip**: "Set to 2:15 AM" (or any time / reset) plans the same request again for the new time and keeps the option the user had started. Moving forward ages every fact step by step; moving back more than a minute starts the night fresh (sources back on). "+5 min" is one atomic step in the engine, so quick taps add up.
* **Scenarios run in their own worker** (about 10 s for eight), so the app keeps ticking while the Demo drawer waits.
* **Real Bluetooth** is shown in the drawer as OFF with the reason (Phase 7, flag `MESH_REAL`).

## UPGRADE U4: no cab, real transit data, transfers

* **Cab / auto removed** from the app (planner, copy, scenarios, tests). The old code is in `legacy/frontend-app-cab/`. The Python reference backend keeps its cab mode; the 4 cab strings it still needs moved to `backend/app/presentation/legacy_copy.json`.
* **Transit data** (docs/TRANSIT_DATA.md): Nagpur Metro is real (OSM relations, 06:00-22:00 every 15 min). No open bus data exists (0 OSM bus routes, no GTFS), so buses are **4 estimated routes E1-E4** on real roads between real places, through the real OSM stops, with assumed headways and **unknown service hours**. Average speeds assumed: bus 6.5 m/s (~23 km/h incl. stops), metro 9.7 m/s.
* **Unknown hours are never invented**: such a ride has an UNKNOWN timing, an expected wait of half the assumed headway *if it runs*, and no worst case; the way only counts as on time in the **best** case, so a way that is expected to work always wins. The user sees "Not sure buses run now." and a Backup. The Demo switch **"Pretend night buses run (simulated)"** gives those routes all-night trips (PROBABLE, labelled simulated) so judges can see transfers.
* **Backup**: another way that does not depend on the weak link, but never a walk beyond the walking limit (e.g. 27 km at night). With no such way, Backup opens **Safe places** (police, hospital, fuel, hotels, pharmacies open now).
* **Transfers**: a RAPTOR-style search over stop clusters (same name within 60 m, or within 25 m), up to 2 changes, walking changes up to 250 m (straight line × 1.3), +5 min per change, expected waits = headway / 2. It proposes up to 4 itineraries with different route sequences; exact times then come from the trips. Tiny walks (< 30 m) between rides are folded into the "Change here" step.
* **Road closed** (police barricade) now blocks walkers too (no own vehicle any more); accidents only add risk. Bus rides do not use street edges, so a closure does not stop a bus.
* **Speed**: "nearest open place" per street segment was brute force (689 ms per 15-min slice on this graph); it is now a grid search capped at 3.5 km (84 ms), identical to brute force within the cap (unit test). Planning IIIT Nagpur → Dharampeth: ~0.4 s in the browser.

## UPGRADE U6: deviation warning

* **Which GPS**: real GPS (Capacitor Geolocation watch, high accuracy) only on a trip that started from a real fix inside the area. A trip started from the demo point uses the Demo drawer's fake GPS; otherwise the dot follows the plan's progress. All on-device; works offline.
* **Thresholds** (config `deviation`): fixes worse than ±50 m are ignored; "off the way" = 3 fixes in a row beyond 60 m **and** 20 s since the first of them; "far" = beyond 250 m (3 fixes) or off for over 3 min; on a bus / metro step 150 m / 500 m; back on the way after 2 good fixes. "I'm fine" quiets **"off"** for 3 min; **"far" is always shown** (a different situation; safety first).
* **"New way"** re-plans from the current fix with the same destination and "Be there by", picks the same tab, starts it, and shows one banner "New way from here."
* **"Are you OK?"** after 5 min without moving 25 m, at night, not on a ride (reuses the safety-timer sheet: I'm fine / I need help).
* **Demo**: "Walk the way" 1× / 10× moves a fake GPS along the way (1.3 m/s × speed); "Wander off" 80 / 300 m **jumps** to a spot that far off (the side farther from the whole way) and stays there. A "Demo time" chip shows in the sheet footer while time or location is simulated.
